This report combines the skills taught in two TIB Academy tutorial programmes: the seven-chapter SAP GRC Access Control Master Course and the 46-lab SAP Controls Full Sweep. It identifies potential junior and associate career pathways, important skills gaps, and the difference between simulated practice and live SAP experience.

Transparency statement: Completing a tutorial does not establish independent competence in a live SAP production system or guarantee employment. Some screens are reconstructed training illustrations, not verified SAP screenshots. SAP navigation and available functionality depend on each customer's configuration and permissions.

This is an indicative curriculum-to-role mapping, not a live vacancy-count ranking, independent accreditation, employer endorsement, or formal certification of every trainee. The course-specific coverage labels require individual workpaper and observed-performance assessment before being awarded to a learner.

1. What the two programmes teach

Course A — SAP GRC Access Control: Access Risk Analysis (ARA), Access Request Management (ARM), Business Role Management (BRM), Emergency Access Management (EAM), User Access Review (UAR), daily GRC consultant activities and interview preparation.

Course B — SAP Controls Full Sweep: 46 interactive controls-testing scenarios covering SAP access, governance, financial and operational processes, evidence evaluation, audit findings, exception handling, reconciliation and remediation.

Together they connect SAP system activity to business risk, control design, test evidence and professional conclusions. Overlapping competencies appear only once in the unified bank below.

5. Three distinct proficiency levels

Level 1 — Foundation

Can explain GRC, access governance, SoD, SOX/ITGC and controls concepts. Evidence: knowledge checks and oral/written explanation.

Level 2 — Simulated Project Practitioner

Can complete relevant tutorial scenarios, document workpapers, investigate exceptions and defend conclusions in a simulated setting. Evidence: assessed lab results, workpapers and case presentations.

Level 3 — SAP System-Validated Practitioner

Can perform specified tasks in an authorized configured SAP environment. Evidence: observed execution, system outputs and assessor sign-off. This level is not automatically conferred by completing the online tutorials.

6. Trainee self-assessment checklist

For each topic, ask: Can I explain the business risk? Can I perform the steps without prompting? Can I interpret the output? Can I produce defensible evidence? Have I done this in a simulator, training SAP system or real client environment?

  • Can I explain and investigate an SoD finding, including its rule-set context?
  • Can I trace an access request from initiation through approval and provisioning checks?
  • Can I distinguish BRM business-role governance from technical role administration?
  • Can I assess Firefighter activity and access certification evidence?
  • Can I test an ITGC or application control, identify exceptions and document a conclusion?
  • Can I reconcile a complete population before choosing a sample?
  • Can I explain which SU01/PFCG, GRC configuration and Fiori tasks I have actually performed in SAP?
  • Can I present an honest skills profile that separates training from employment experience?

Recommended trainee outcome: Keep a portfolio of assessed workpapers and lab outcomes. Do not describe simulated tasks as production implementation experience.
